# Presenter Workflow → Atlas Monetization Playbook

## Operating rule

This playbook reproduces the presenter's start-to-finish sales method as a
supervised Atlas experiment. “Presenter-reported” means observed in the pitch,
not independently validated. The presenter promotes an affiliate-linked platform,
trial, snapshot, masterclass, and paid upgrade
(`video-qy0l1t7x6le-affiliate-conflict`), so product, price, and outcome claims
remain hypotheses. Atlas policy, regional review, customer terms, and measured
results govern execution.

## Stage 0 — Choose region and niche

1. Select one active country pack (first focus: USA/North America/Caribbean).
2. Select one local-service niche with a clear website/contact job.
3. Write a market hypothesis: customer problem, weak-site pattern, expected
   website benefit, directory coverage, likely channel, and disconfirming signal.
4. Research 20–50 businesses without contacting them: current sites, GBP/profile
   quality, offers, reviews, competitors, customer language, and local norms.
5. Research provider terms, contact-data provenance, channel policy, currency,
   offer terms/tax questions, cost-to-serve, and alternative providers.
6. Approve/reject the market brief. Unresolved policy means research-only.

Deliverable: versioned regional market brief with evidence quality and expiry.
Never infer a legal conclusion from the video.

## Stage 1 — Source and qualify prospects

Presenter method (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-google-maps-prospecting`): search Google
Maps by industry + location and find an active profile with no website link;
optionally ask a research agent for 10–20 candidates.

Atlas method:

1. Use an approved directory adapter or manual research.
2. Capture business name, profile URL, location, categories, hours, phone,
   reviews, photos/social links, website link, retrieval time, and source.
3. Classify website status:
   - `none`: no website link and no independently verified official site.
   - `weak`: official site exists with documented observable defects.
   - `adequate`: no clear website need; disqualify from this offer.
   - `unknown`: conflict or stale evidence; hold.
4. Score fit, evidence, contactability, demo effort, potential benefit, and risk.
5. Record contact source, provider/region policy decision, suppression, owner, and
   expiry. Public data is neither asset permission nor contact consent.
6. Human operator marks `qualified` or reasoned `disqualified`.

Deliverable: sourced prospect dossier and qualification assessment.

## Stage 2 — Capture the business descriptor

Presenter method (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-gbp-site-prompt`): use GBP/Maps, reviews,
photos, social profiles, and public information in a premium-site prompt.

Atlas method:

1. Fetch approved source documents with timestamp/hash.
2. Extract normalized facts with source/confidence; flag contradictions.
3. Classify assets as owner-supplied, licensed/authorized, link-only, or unusable.
4. Operator verifies identity, contact, address/service area, hours, services,
   claims, reviews/testimonials, and CTA.
5. Create a versioned JSON descriptor: navigation, pages/sections, sourced copy,
   fact/asset refs, tokens, locale, structured data, CTA and event bindings.
6. Use explicit placeholders for gaps; never invent certifications, pricing,
   availability, results, testimonials, or owner endorsement.

Deliverable: verified research dossier + editable descriptor.

## Stage 3 — Prebuild five to ten demos

Presenter method (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-prebuild-demos`): build 5 or 10 tailored
sites before outreach.

Atlas method:

1. Cap the queue at 10; record expected effort/value and expiry.
2. Choose a versioned vertical template or assisted descriptor workflow
   (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-template-library`,
   `video-qy0l1t7x6le-ai-assisted-editor`).
3. Render deterministically from descriptor + template + style tokens.
4. Run factual, rights, accessibility, responsive, links, forms, security,
   privacy, structured-data, performance, and browser checks.
5. Fix/block every release-severity failure.
6. Create private/noindex, expiring preview; record build hash/cost/time.
7. Human reviews for truth, quality, fit, non-deception, and outreach readiness.

Deliverable: 5–10 approved, expiring demo links. The video supplies no evidence
that this volume improves conversion; Atlas measures labor and outcomes.

## Stage 4 — Share value in advance

Presenter method (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-multichannel-outreach`): email the concept,
then SMS, social DM, and phone call.

Atlas supervised pilot:

1. Draft a short message identifying the sender, why the business was selected,
   that the preview is unsolicited/a concept, its expiry, and a simple next step.
2. Resolve channel availability and policy from the region pack; verify source,
   suppression, contact window, caps, identity, content hash, and offer version.
3. Human approves each recipient/channel/content/window separately.
4. Send email first only if eligible; reconcile delivery.
5. Stop on reply, opt-out, complaint, invalid address, business closure, or risk.
6. Only after a reviewed no-reply state may Atlas draft the next eligible channel.
   SMS, social DM, and phone never fire automatically in the pilot.
7. Log the exact touch, provider receipt, outcome, and human notes.

Deliverable: complete, suppressible touch history. Atlas does not adopt the
presenter's frequency as compliant or effective without regional evidence.

## Stage 5 — Discovery and offer

Presenter method (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-free-hosting-offer`): give away the site/site
concept and charge for hosting—value first, then recurring revenue.

Atlas method:

1. Confirm owner identity, business goals, content changes, domain/asset rights,
   contact integrations, support needs, and success measure.
2. Present preview vs production scope and an explicit offer version.
3. Disclose site/domain ownership, export/migration, hosting/security, uptime
   boundary, support and edit pricing, integrations, data handling, renewal,
   taxes, cancellation/refund, suspension, and handoff.
4. Owner approves final content and terms; operator approves production version.
5. Payment provider confirms payment/mandate; Atlas stores provider references,
   not card data.
6. Activate hosting entitlement, verify domain/deployment fingerprint/health,
   provide export/support contacts, and record baseline.

Deliverable: active hosting customer or a recorded decline. Free-site economics
must be validated; “free” must not conceal lock-in or future obligations.

## Presenter-reported price hypotheses

Evidence `video-qy0l1t7x6le-presenter-pricing` records:

- USD 100/month hosting plus tax in one example.
- USD 119/month hosting with security/chat in another.
- USD 2,000 website reference; USD 2,000 SEO; USD 2,000 reputation.
- USD 300–2,500/month social, depending on volume/platforms.
- USD 1,000/month email.
- USD 50/hour basic and USD 100/hour complex edits.
- Caption-rendered USD 9.97/month AEO, requiring audio/offer verification.

None is an Atlas price. Before a regional offer: validate customer problem and
willingness to pay, competitive alternatives, provider and labor costs, support,
tax/terms, currency, margin, cancellation risk, and a small approved experiment.

## Stage 6 — Diagnose and sequence upsells

After hosting is stable, conduct discovery and baseline—not a scripted hard sell.

1. **Reputation:** review coverage/response workflow; integrate monitor and keep
   responses human-approved (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-upsell-reputation`).
2. **SEO:** technical/local audit with measured findings, priorities, and no
   ranking guarantee (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-upsell-seo`).
3. **AEO:** observable citations/entity consistency/structured data with explicit
   uncertainty (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-upsell-aeo`).
4. **Social:** channel/content/rights/calendar baseline; draft/approve before
   provider publishing (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-upsell-social`).
5. **Email:** only provenance-tagged, consent-valid contacts with suppression,
   domain readiness, preview and approval (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-upsell-email`).
6. **Ads:** only with account authorization, landing page, conversion tracking,
   approved currency budget, hard cap, and kill switch
   (`video-qy0l1t7x6le-upsell-ads`).

Offer only the diagnosed capability with scope, entitlement, price version,
baseline, measurable success, review date, renewal, and cancellation.

## Stage 7 — Analytics and learning loop

For every cohort record:

`market approved → prospects qualified → demos approved → touches approved/sent
→ replies → meetings → offers → hosting activations → retention → service attach`.

Also record time/cost/QA, source freshness, corrections, approval rejection,
delivery, suppression/complaint, support, provider cost, gross margin, churn, and
customer-reported value. Compare region, niche, template, channel, sequence and
offer versions. Do not treat last-touch correlation as causation.

At a scheduled review:

1. compare results with predeclared success/failure thresholds;
2. review complaints, policy, quality, margin, and support;
3. promote, revise, pause, or reject the hypothesis;
4. update evidence, playbook, candidate lifecycle, region pack, and work queue;
5. retain de-identified learning and exact next action for the next model.

## Definition of complete pilot

- One region/niche brief approved.
- Every prospect and fact has provenance and policy status.
- Five-to-ten cap, QA, preview expiry, and approvals enforced.
- Every outbound touch was shadow-generated, human-approved, suppressible, audited.
- Hosting terms/payment/entitlement/deployment were verified.
- One paying customer completes the path, or failure is recorded transparently.
- Cost, outcome, risk, and next experiment are committed to the control system.
